# I. Player Rules

## 1. Character Creation

Follow these steps in order to build a character from scratch.

**Step 1 — Choose a Clan and determine your Ability.**
Ten clans are available (full traits in Section 3): **Kith, Ctarl, Kobold, Goblin, Zora, Rito, Kreen, Ygguana, Garo, Ronso.** Clan is fully independent of class — any pairing is valid. Choosing your clan also settles your Ability (Section 2) — Chimera get their clan's fixed trait, Kith roll for one.

**Step 2 — Choose a Class.**
Seven are available: **Hunter, Delver, Caster, Priest, Shaman, Ranger, Monk.** Full writeups are in Section 4. Pick based on how you want to spend your turns in combat and out of it — and feel free to let your clan or ability from Step 1 point you toward one.

**Step 3 — Determine starting Hit Points.**
Each class lists a base HP value (Section 4). That's your total at 1st level.

**Step 4 — Determine starting MP, if any.**
Caster and Shaman draw from the larger Magic Points progression; Priest and Monk draw from a smaller one (full numbers in Section 7). Hunter, Delver, and Ranger have none.

**Step 5 — Take your starting equipment.**
Every character starts with the same baseline regardless of class: rations (1 slot), torches (1 slot), and a small coin purse (1 slot) for incidentals. On top of that, each class has a ready-made loadout (Section 5) built to fit the remaining slots and that class's weapon/armor rules — take it as written, or swap items of equal slot cost within your class's restrictions.

**Step 6 — Name, clan, and a line of history.**
No mechanical weight, but worth having — a sentence or two of who this person was before the dungeon found them.

## 2. Abilities

A character's ability is the single trait that sets them apart from an ordinary person of their class and clan — a defining strength beyond what their class and clan already grant.

**Getting your ability:**

- **If you're Chimera** (any clan but Kith): your clan's fixed trait (Section 3) *is* your defining ability — there's nothing further to roll or pick. This is the Chimera's core payoff: a guaranteed, specific strength instead of a gamble.
- **If you're Kith:** roll 2d6 on the table below. The chance at a double result is Kith's own payoff for not getting a fixed trait.

| 2d6 | Ability | Lets you attempt |
|---|---|---|
| 2-3 | Strength | Bend bars, lift gates, force doors |
| 4 | Constitution | Withstand adversity, hold breath, swim rough water |
| 5-6 | Wisdom | Spot clues, detect lies, track |
| 7 | Dexterity | Climb walls, hide/sneak, steal small objects |
| 8-9 | Charisma | Lead and persuade others |
| 10-11 | Intelligence | Recall lore and legends |
| 12 | Any two abilities | Choose both, or roll twice more |

Your ability governs:

- **Non-combat actions** (Section 11) — a character attempting an action tied to their ability rolls at standard odds; a character without the ability can still try, but at a steep penalty, and some actions may simply be closed to them at the referee's discretion.
- **Flavor and roleplay** — how your character is exceptional, independent of class. A Strength Caster and an Intelligence Caster read very differently at the table even though their spell list is identical.

**The six abilities, in full:**

- **Strength** — physical power. Bend iron bars, lift a portcullis, force open a stuck door.
- **Constitution** — health and endurance. Holding breath, swimming rough water, shrugging off adversity; a character with this ability gets +1 on saving dice against disease or poison.
- **Dexterity** — speed, balance, coordination. Climbing, sneaking, sleight of hand.
- **Wisdom** — intuition and perception. Spotting clues, noticing an ambush, telling when someone's lying.
- **Charisma** — force of personality. Leading, persuading, striking deals.
- **Intelligence** — knowledge and recall. Knowing the name of an artifact on sight, recalling a relevant legend, recognizing a magical effect from lore rather than trial and error. A character with Intelligence can also read scrolls and decipher magic command words, regardless of class (Section 9) — Caster, Shaman, Priest, and Monk can already do both through their training and don't need the ability for it.

## 3. Kith & Chimera (Clans)

Clan is fully independent of class — any of the ten below can pair with any of the seven classes in Section 4. Every clan with claws or fangs fights unarmed as a natural one-handed weapon, no penalty.

Chimera clans think of themselves in terms of clan and tribe — a Ctarl calls themselves part of the **Ctarl Clan**, and so on down the list. Kith don't think in those terms about themselves, but Chimera commonly refer to Kith collectively as the "**Kith Clan**" anyway, whether Kith like it or not.

| Clan | Trait | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Kith | No fixed trait — roll for your ability instead of having one guaranteed (Section 2) | — |
| Ctarl | The party gains +1 to its initiative roll whenever a Ctarl is present (Section 10); may roll saving dice to take no fall damage where a cat-like landing is plausible (referee's call), otherwise the standard half-damage-on-save applies | Water aversion — genuine discomfort in or near water |
| Kobold | Can read scrolls and decipher magic command words; finds hidden doors, passages, and stonework oddities at a glance (2-in-6, instead of the default 1-in-6); +1 saving dice, +2 vs. mechanical traps | Uncomfortable in open, sunlit surface terrain |
| Goblin | Detects movement in total darkness within 30' by echolocation | Loud noise negates it |
| Zora | Innately, unaccountably charismatic to other species — a universal magnetism no one can quite explain — plus empathy and telepathic communication with any mind within 30' underwater. Water-breathing and effortless swimming are just fictional fact for a Zora, not part of the trait | On land, the telepathy and emotion-sensing collapse to touch range only |
| Rito | Flies at full speed | Only possible with 4 free pack slots — flight requires traveling light |
| Kreen | Regenerates a lost limb within 1d6 days (flavor); heals +1 HP per day even without resting (mechanical) | Alien, unsettling appearance — some NPCs react warily on sight |
| Ygguana | Immune to poison and disease from mundane sources or weak creatures — strong swimmer and can hold their breath a long time as a matter of course, never troubled by drowning rules | Cold-blooded: sluggish and slowed in cold environments |
| Garo | Detects hidden creatures by scent within 30' in darkness or fog; cannot be surprised by anything with a scent | Strong or overpowering smells nearby disrupt or distract them |
| Ronso | Fights with both claws as natural weapons for +1 damage, dividing that damage among multiple targets in melee range (Ambidexterity, applied racially) | No shield, and no more than leather armor, while fighting this way |

*Ctarl's rare shifter variant — a beast-form transformation some Ctarl can access — is still being designed and isn't available yet.*

## 4. Classes

A quick guide if you're not sure where to start:

| If you want to... | Play |
|---|---|
| Hit things hard and often, wear whatever you want | Hunter |
| Sneak, steal, and land a devastating single strike | Delver |
| Cast the widest range of spells and craft magic items | Caster |
| Fight and cast in whatever mix suits you, growing either kit as you level | Priest |
| Cast nature magic and handle wild creatures | Shaman |
| Fight from range, track, and strike from ambush | Ranger |
| Fight bare-handed and channel magic through your fists | Monk |

**Combatant** classes fight on the Combatant attack table (Section 10); **Non-combatant** classes fight on the gentler Non-combatant table. Combat moves and their full effects are detailed in Section 10 — this section only lists which moves each class can access and when.

### Hunter
- **HP:** 10, +2/level. **Table:** Combatant.
- **Armor/weapons:** any.
- **Attack bonus:** +1 on all attack rolls, from 1st level.
- **Combat moves:** full list, gaining 1 at 1st, a 2nd at 3rd, a 3rd at 6th (one active per round). 9th level is reserved for the feature below, not another move.
- **9th level:** two melee attacks per round, against a single opponent or divided between targets in range.

### Delver
- **HP:** 8, +1/level. **Table:** Non-combatant.
- **Armor/weapons:** leather only; any one-handed weapon.
- **Stealth:** +1 every 3 levels, up to +3 at 9th. Always counts as having Dexterity for non-combat actions (Section 11), regardless of personal ability — this matters most before 3rd level, when the stealth bonus hasn't kicked in yet.
- **Backstab:** an attack roll at +2 to hit, constant from 1st level. On a hit, deal flat damage instead of rolling — the Non-combatant table's max (2) times a multiplier that grows with level: ×1 at 1st (2 flat hits), ×2 at 3rd (4), ×3 at 6th (6), ×4 at 9th (8).
- **Magic item gambit:** see Section 9.
- **Combat moves:** none.
- **Side:** can never be Lawful (Section 10).

### Caster
- **HP:** 6, +1/level. **Table:** Non-combatant.
- **Armor/weapons:** none; dagger or staff, or any one-handed weapon.
- **Magic:** draws from the Ancient tradition list (Section 8) — the largest of the three, and the widest MP pool of any class. MP starts at 2 and rises by 2 every odd level (2/4/6/8/10 at 1st/3rd/5th/7th/9th); gains a new spell every level.
- **Crafting:** unlocked in stages — potions and scrolls at 3rd, rings/staves/wands at 6th, armor/weapons/mixed magic at 9th (see Section 5 for costs).
- **Item use:** full access to and crafting of magic items of every kind.

### Priest
- **HP:** 8, +1/level. **Table:** Non-combatant.
- **Armor/weapons:** any weapon; shield allowed; up to chainmail. No stealth of any kind.
- **Combat moves or spells:** at 3rd, 6th, and 9th level, choose independently — a combat move (full list, Section 10) or a new spell. A Priest can end up anywhere from a nearly-full move list with a handful of spells to the reverse, or any mix between.
- **Magic:** draws from the Holy tradition list (Section 8), with a smaller MP pool and Elf-rate acquisition. Every spell a Priest knows always resolves at its 1st-level duration/effect, regardless of the Priest's own level — this holds even for a spell learned at 9th level.
- **Item use:** Holy-tradition scrolls, wands, staves, and rings (Section 9).
- **Side:** can never be Neutral (Section 10).

A Priest is a rune-scholar who never stopped being a fighter, or a fighter who never stopped studying — which end of that a given Priest leans toward is entirely a matter of how they've spent their milestones. Most belong to a temple or order; the largest and oldest of these is the **Seekers**, devoted to **Ultima** — a distant, ambiguous figure venerated as a goddess, an ancestor, or something not yet fully understood. Seekers believe their rites are Ultima's own gift, and that recovering enough of the old world's runes and ruins will one day lead their people home. Other orders exist, devoted to other figures — elemental spirits, ancient machine-minds, undying beings — and a Priest's class features work identically regardless of which order they've sworn to.

### Shaman
- **HP:** 8, +1/level. **Table:** Non-combatant.
- **Armor/weapons:** none; staff only.
- **Magic:** draws from the Spirit tradition list (Section 8), with the same MP formula as Caster (starts at 2, +2 every odd level, new spell every level).
- **Wildlife:** can freely attempt to pacify or repel wild creatures at any time, no cost.
- **Item use:** full use of magic items, but no crafting.

### Ranger
- **HP:** 8, +2/level. **Table:** Combatant, no roll bonus.
- **Armor/weapons:** no heavy armor or shields; no two-handed melee weapons (ranged two-handers are fine, and carry the usual two-handed +1).
- **Tracking:** +1 every 3 levels — the wilderness-scoped equivalent of Delver's stealth: sneaking, spotting hazards and trail-sign, wilderness traps.
- **Sneak attack (ranged only):** an attack roll at +2 to hit, constant from 1st level. On a hit, deal flat damage instead of rolling — the Combatant table's max (3) plus the two-handed weapon bonus (+1) = 4, times a multiplier that grows with level: ×1 at 1st (4 flat hits), ×2 at 3rd (8), ×3 at 6th (12), ×4 at 9th (16) — the highest non-magical single-hit ceiling of any class.
- **Combat moves:** Dodge and Volley only, both gained at 3rd level, one active per round.

### Monk
- **HP:** 8, +2/level. **Table:** Combatant.
- **Armor/weapons:** none; bare hands or quarterstaff.
- **Combat moves:** Dodge, Block, Parry, Smash, and Sweep only (Sweep needs a quarterstaff), same 3rd/6th/9th schedule, one active per round.
- **Fighting style:** bare hands deal +1 damage, a quarterstaff deals +2 — but only bare-handed strikes can deliver a spell.
- **Spell-on-hit:** on a successful bare-handed hit, choose normal damage or spend 1 MP to deliver a declared touch/self spell instead. A natural 12 does both, and the spell costs nothing that round.
- **Magic:** draws from the Spirit tradition list (Section 8), restricted to self/touch-range effects, declared before initiative like every other caster. A smaller MP pool, and new spells only at 3rd, 6th, and 9th level, same rate as Priest (Shaman is a full caster like Caster, gaining a spell every level instead).

See Magic Traditions, below, for what each class's tradition restricts them to.

### Magic Traditions

Every caster-adjacent class — Caster, Shaman, Priest, Monk — is restricted to its own tradition's spell list (Section 8). A second, independent axis exists alongside tradition: **element** (fire, wind, earth, water, ice, lightning, dark, light, plants, beasts, and others). Element is pure flavor — it never determines what a class can or can't learn.

**Ancient.** Caster's tradition. Magic as a studied, stolen, or reconstructed body of knowledge — hidden laws of the world, written down and argued over, including the darker corners of that knowledge: curses, forbidden life-and-death magic, and things better left buried. Ancient casters tend toward guilds, masters and apprentices, and old rivalries over who found which secret first.

**Spirit.** Shaman and Monk's tradition. Magic as a relationship rather than a subject — bonds formed with elemental or primal forces, briefly hosted or channeled rather than studied. A Monk channels this through discipline of the body; a Shaman channels it through the wilds and the creatures in them.

**Holy.** Priest's tradition. Magic as rite and inheritance — passed down through an order, credited to a venerated source rather than personal study or private bond. The Seekers, devoted to Ultima, are the most widespread holy order, but the tradition stays open to others devoted to different elemental spirits, ancient machine-minds, or undying beings.

A referee or player is free to invent new orders or patrons within any tradition; the mechanics never change.

## 5. Equipment & Treasure

**Universal starting kit** (every character, regardless of class): rations, torches, a coin purse (Tier I) to spend on incidentals, keep, or bank in town. Rope, a bedroll, and similar extras are optional — bundle up to three small items per slot if you want to carry them.

Each class below lists a ready-made starting loadout. Take it as written, or swap items of equal slot cost within your class's weapon/armor rules.

**Hunter** — any armor, any weapon
- Chainmail, plus a choice of: shield + one-handed melee weapon of your choice, **or** a two-handed melee weapon of your choice

**Delver** — leather only, any one-handed weapon
- Leather armor, any two one-handed weapons of your choice (melee or ranged — a shortsword and pistol, dual pistols, dual daggers, whatever fits the character), lockpicks

**Caster** — no armor, dagger/staff or any one-handed weapon
- Staff, spellbook

**Priest** — any weapon, shield allowed, up to chainmail
- Chainmail, plus a choice of: shield + one-handed weapon of your choice, **or** two one-handed weapons (a mace and a shield, dual pistols, whatever fits the character)

**Shaman** — no armor, staff only
- Staff

**Ranger** — no heavy armor/shields, no two-handed melee (ranged two-handers fine)
- Rifle or carbine, leather armor, a one-handed backup weapon of your choice — kept in a body slot for instant access, or in the pack instead (costs a round to retrieve)

**Monk** — no armor, bare hands or quarterstaff
- Bare-handed by default (needed to deliver spell-on-hit), or a quarterstaff if you'd rather have the extra damage

### Usage Dice

A handful of everyday supplies are tracked with a single die instead of an exact count — the kind of thing a real adventurer would eyeball rather than tally precisely. Every time you use one of these items, roll its die:

- Roll its current size and it comes up **1 or 2**: the die steps down one size (d12 → d10 → d8 → d6 → d4 → gone).
- Roll anything else: no change. Use it again next time exactly the same way.

When a supply's die is gone, that slot is empty — you're out until you restock in town or find more.

| Item | Starting die |
|---|---|
| Torches | d6 |
| Rations | d8 |
| Ammunition (arrows, bolts, sling stones) | d12 |
| Healing herbs | d6 |
| Lantern oil | d10 |
| Any other incidental consumable | d6 (default) |

**Torches and lanterns track supply, not burn time.** The die only tracks how many torches (or lantern refills) are left in your pack — roll it when you light a fresh one, not every turn it's lit. How long any single torch stays lit is handled by the fiction, the same way marching order and who's holding the light already are: a light source can be dropped, doused, or knocked out at the moment it matters (see Combat Moves, Section 10, for the penalty on dropping a two-handed light source mid-fight), but nothing ticks down in the background while it burns.

**Magic item charges are the opposite case, deliberately.** MP and the charges on wands/staves (Section 9) stay exact numbers, not dice. A character would realistically lose count of how many torches or rations are left in the pack — but not how many shots are left on a fireball wand. The imprecision that makes usage dice a good fit for mundane supplies is exactly the wrong fit for a resource whose precise count matters for a tactical decision.

## 6. Advancement

Characters don't advance by counting kills or gold. A character reaches a new level by completing a milestone appropriate to their class and story — earning a guild rank, training an apprentice, passing a trial suited to what they do. There's no other path to a level: a player who never pursues the milestone simply never reaches that level. Nothing else needs to enforce that; it falls out naturally from there being only one road in.

What a level actually grants — HP, MP, combat moves, spells, crafting access — is exactly what's listed under each class in Section 4. Advancement only changes *how* you get there, never what's waiting at the top.

A milestone's completion is always narrated as part of a downtime beat (Section 13), not a separate transaction bolted onto one — the guild ceremony, the lesson from a mentor, the training montage between adventures. Leveling up happens in town, in the story, not the instant the last die lands in a dungeon.

**Player characters cap at 10th level.**

# II. Magic Rules

## 7. Spell Casting

The actual mechanics of casting are unchanged from the base game. The one real departure is when a spell has to be committed to — everything else below is the original system running as-is.

**Cost.** Most spells cost 1 MP to cast. A handful cost more — the cost is stated directly on the spell.

**Running dry.** Once a caster's MP pool is fully spent, they can't cast again until they recover some back. Magic items — scrolls, wands, staves, rings — still work perfectly normally even when their user is completely out of personal MP; item charges are a separate resource from a caster's own pool.

**Recovery.** MP comes back at a rate of 1 per full day of rest, and that day has to be spent on nothing more strenuous than rest and quiet study — no adventuring, no travel, no fighting. This is the same rest that recovers HP (Section 12) and ties directly into the Rest & Recover downtime activity (Section 13).

**Declaring a spell.** Every spell is declared **before initiative is rolled**, not cast as a normal action on your turn. This applies to every caster in the game, at every level, with no exceptions — a visible telegraph rather than a surprise. It's what makes a boss's signature spell (Section 17) something a party can actually react to, and it applies just as much to a 1st-level Caster's first-ever spell as it does to an archdemon's.

**Using magic items.** Reading a scroll, activating a wand, or drawing on a staff never costs MP — the item supplies its own power. Whether a class can use a given kind of magic item at all is set by that class's entry in Section 4.

## 9. Magic Items

**Item traditions.** Every scroll, staff, wand, and ring belongs to one of the three Magic Traditions (Section 4): Ancient, Spirit, or Holy. Which classes can use a given item is set entirely by its tradition tag: Caster uses Ancient items, Shaman and Monk use Spirit items, Priest uses Holy items. A class never uses an item outside its own tradition.

**Scrolls.** Reading one performs the spell as if cast by a 9th-level Caster, and the writing fades after a single use. Usable by Caster/Shaman/Priest/Monk within their own tradition (they're assumed literate through training), or by **any character, regardless of class, whose personal ability is Intelligence** (Section 2) — reading a scroll doesn't require understanding the tradition behind it, just the ability to follow written instruction. A Delver without Intelligence can still attempt one — see the Magic Item Gambit, below. Only an NPC specialist can write a scroll.

**Command words.** Some magic items require a spoken word, inscribed somewhere on the item, to activate. Deciphering one follows the same access rule as Scrolls, above.

**Potions.** Single-use, no MP cost, functions as the spell it's named for cast at 9th level unless its own description says otherwise. Potions carry no tradition tag and no class restriction — anyone can drink one, no roll required.

**Staves.** Carry a limited number of charges (25 is the default unless a specific staff says otherwise). Recharging a spent staff costs 10% of its total value, whether the owner does it themselves (by casting spells directly into it) or pays an NPC caster to do it. Restricted to Caster/Shaman/Priest/Monk by tradition tag, same as Scrolls — Intelligence does not open staff access to other classes; a Delver may still attempt one via the Magic Item Gambit.

**Wands.** Function the same way as staves unless a specific wand's description says otherwise. Same tradition-tag restriction as Staves.

**Rings.** No universal mechanic established yet — each ring's effect is unique and self-contained; treat case-by-case until a general rule proves necessary. Same tradition-tag restriction as Staves once a general mechanic exists.

**Mixed magic.** A catch-all category for items that don't fit the above — each is its own unique effect, usable by whichever classes its description allows.

### Delver's Magic Item Gambit

A Delver without Intelligence can still attempt to activate any magic item outside the classes above — scrolls, staves, wands, rings, regardless of tradition — by rolling the standard Dexterity-based non-combat action (Section 11), including Delver's own stealth bonus (+1 every 3 levels). This is the only way for a Chimera Delver, who can never roll or choose Intelligence (Section 2), to access magic items at all — a Kith Delver has the same option available whether or not their rolled ability happens to be Intelligence.

A failed attempt triggers backlash — roll 2d6:

| 2d6 | Result |
|---|---|
| 2 | **Volatile mishandling.** 1d6 damage per item Tier (Section 5). |
| 3-4 | **Power drain.** The item's cost is drained straight from the Delver's HP instead of its own reserve — HP loss equal to what the attempt would have cost in charges (1 HP for a scroll's single use). |
| 5-9 | **Fizzle.** Nothing happens; the use/charge is wasted regardless. |
| 10-11 | **Miscast.** The effect fires on the wrong target (self, a nearby ally, or whoever's closest), or, for a multi-effect item, an unintended effect goes off instead. |
| 12 | **Overcharged** (staves/wands only). The effect fires at double potency or duration, but burns 2 charges instead of 1 — good or bad depending on the spell and the situation. **Scrolls:** same as Fizzle, since there's no second charge to overdraw. |

This roll only applies to items outside a Delver's normal access — Caster/Shaman/Priest/Monk using an item within their own tradition never rolls for backlash, and neither does anyone using a potion.

# III. Game Rules

## 10. Combat

### Attack Tables

Every attack is a 2d6 roll. Which table you use depends on your class.

| Table | Miss | 1 hit | 2 hits | 3 hits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Non-combatant | 2-7 | 8-9 | 10-12 | — |
| Combatant | 2-7 | 8-9 | 10-11 | 12 |
| Hunter (Combatant +1) | 2-6 | 7-8 | 9-10 | 11+ |

- **Combatant:** Hunter, Ranger, Monk.
- **Non-combatant:** Delver, Caster, Priest, Shaman.
- **Monsters** default to the Combatant table; the referee can override this per creature.
- A **natural 2 always misses**, no matter what bonuses apply.
- A **natural 12** still triggers special effects independent of the table — vorpal blades, hammer of thunder, Smash/Thrust bonus damage, and similar.

### Weapons and Magic

- A **magic weapon's bonus** applies to the attack roll itself, shifting which tier a hit lands in — it stacks with any class-based bonus to the roll.
- A **two-handed weapon** (great axe, great sword, flail, morning star, long/short bow, crossbow, sling, firearm) adds a flat +1 to the final hit count instead, and costs the user their shield.
- Using a weapon off-label (swinging a bow like a club) keeps the normal handling requirement but only ever deals one-handed damage.
- Firearms work exactly like bows: two-handed, +1 damage, usable with Volley.

### Armor

Armor adds a flat bonus to your total hits — it never touches the attack roll. Leather +1, chainmail +2, plate +3, shield +1, and these stack.

A magic weapon bonus and a magic armor bonus are not the same thing: a magic weapon shifts the *attacker's* roll, while magic armor just makes the *wearer's* armor act as if rated higher (e.g., +2 magic chainmail behaves like chainmail +4). Neither one is a modifier to the other.

Armor points absorb damage first, before base HP — but only from attacks: weapon hits, bites, claws, stings, falls, and non-lethal subdual. Ongoing poison, disease, and drowning damage bypass armor and hit base HP directly.

Armor resets to full after any day of rest, regardless of how depleted it was. Base HP still recovers at its normal rate (Section 12).

### Combat Moves

Combatant classes with move access gain 1 move at 3rd level, a 2nd at 6th, a 3rd at 9th, except Hunter (1st/3rd/6th, with 9th reserved for its own feature). Only one move is active per round.

Priest is the one Non-combatant class with move access — see Section 4's spell-or-move choice.

| Maneuver | Requirements | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Abjure | Any spellcasting class | On a natural 12 in melee, cast a known spell instead of dealing damage, even if magic was already used that round |
| Aim | Bow, mounted | +1 to the first shot; no penalty firing from a moving mount |
| Bash | Shield | Knock a foe back |
| Berserk | Chaos or Neutral Side | Below half HP, a Ward-spell-equivalent, once per day |
| Block | Shield | Deflect an attack |
| Bracing | Spear | Set against a charge; the charger rolls saving dice or takes damage |
| Charges | Mounted, warhorse, full speed | +1 to the first attack |
| Cheat | Chaos Side | On a natural 12 in melee, an extra attack at -2 |
| Cover | Shield | Ignore missile fire (can't fight while doing this) |
| Snipe | Bow or firearm | Bonus damage on a natural 12 |
| Dodge | 20' of space | Deflect an attack, no shield needed |
| Hew Foes | One-handed bladed weapon, no shield | On a natural 12, a full second attack at -1 against a different target |
| Inspire | Law Side | On a successful hit, forgo damage and rally a henchman within 60' to your side, once per encounter |
| Parry | Melee weapon | Deflect or disarm |
| Smash | Axe or hammer | Bonus damage on a natural 12 |
| Specialize | One-handed weapon | +1 with one chosen weapon |
| Sweep | Two-handed melee weapon | Divide a hit's damage among multiple foes in range |
| Thrust | Blade or spear | Bonus damage on a natural 12 |
| Volley | Bow, sling, or firearm | Divide a hit's damage among multiple foes in range |

### Side

Every character holds a personal ethic — Law, Chaos, or Neutral — that governs which Side-gated combat moves they can use and colors how NPCs react to them.

- **Law** values order and the collective.
- **Chaos** values individual freedom and self-reliance.
- **Neutral** is pragmatic, uncommitted to either.

Delver can never be Lawful. Priest can never be Neutral. All other classes may choose freely.

### Initiative

Each side rolls 1d6 at the start of combat. Higher roll acts first, and that side keeps acting first every round until the fight ends — no re-roll each round.

**Modifiers:**

| Condition | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Surprised or unprepared | -2 |
| Heavily outnumbered | -1 |
| A Ctarl is present in the party | +1 |

Re-roll initiative if conditions change significantly mid-fight — one side drops below half its starting numbers, a leader dies, reinforcements arrive.

### Non-Lethal Combat

Attacking to subdue rather than kill requires a blunt weapon and only works against Kith-sized, living targets — never automatons or undead. Combat resolves normally, but any result of 12 or better forces the target to roll saving dice (at +2) or fall unconscious for 1d6 rounds.

### Evasion and Pursuit

A fleeing party can distract pursuers by dropping food or valuables. Animals stop to eat it roughly 1-in-6 of the time; intelligent enemies stop for dropped treasure about 1-in-3, unless they have a stronger reason to keep chasing (revenge, orders). Any pursuer acts in its own interest — badly outnumbered or heavily wounded enemies break off rather than press a fight they'd lose.

## 11. Movement, Encumbrance, Non-Combat Actions

### Movement

| Armor | Move | Restrictions |
|---|---|---|
| Unarmored | 50'/round | None |
| Leather | 40'/round | None |
| Chainmail | 30'/round | No stealth; swims at half speed |
| Plate mail | 20'/round | Can't swim or use stealth at all |
| Shield | No penalty | No bows or two-handed melee while equipped |

A character in plate who ends up in the water must roll saving dice every round (cumulative -1 each round) or start drowning. Anyone in armor heavy enough to risk drowning takes 1 hit per round until rescued or they make it out. Water-adapted clans (Section 3) simply never trigger any of this.

### Encumbrance

Handled entirely by the 10-slot system in Section 5 — there's no separate weight or GP-based encumbrance limit layered on top of it.

### Non-Combat Actions

Rolled at 8+ on 2d6, modified by conditions. A character attempting an action tied to their own ability rolls at this standard rate; one without the ability can still try, but only at the referee's discretion and a steep penalty.

**Delver always counts as having Dexterity**, regardless of what ability they rolled or chose — they can attempt any Dexterity-linked action at the standard rate no matter what. If a Delver's own personal ability also happens to be Dexterity, they get an additional +1 to all stealth-related dice.

| Lift gates | Strength | 1d6 rds | — |

| Steal objects | Dexterity | — | — |

**Modifiers:**

| Condition | Modifier |
|---|---|

### Falling

1d6 damage per 20' fallen. Saving dice halve it. Ctarl (Section 3) can roll to avoid it entirely where a cat-like landing makes sense.

### Traps

Anyone with Dexterity can attempt to find and disarm a manmade trap before triggering it. Otherwise, a triggered trap requires saving dice: success means no damage (half damage for falling traps specifically). Some traps capture rather than harm — a victim is helpless until rescued, but usually keeps access to weapons or magic depending on the trap.

### Dungeon Time & Random Encounters

Exploration runs in **turns** (roughly 10 minutes); combat runs in **rounds**. Movement underground is 120'/turn.

Check for a random encounter every 2 turns of exploring — 1-in-6 chance. Never run two random encounters in consecutive checks. Draw from the Bestiary's lower tiers (Level 1-3) for these; they should be weak and rarely worth fighting for treasure. Over a typical 4-6 hour session this produces 2-4 encounters on average — enough real attrition to matter, in keeping with HP being a whole-dungeon resource (Section 12) rather than a per-fight one.

## 12. Wounds, Death, and Recovery

HP represents stamina, health, and wounds all at once — there's no separate injury or condition layer sitting underneath it. A character reduced to 0 HP is dead or grievously incapacitated, full stop. Hit point pools are generous and grow every level specifically so that reaching 0 can mean something real without every individual fight feeling arbitrarily punishing.

**The actual resource a dungeon crawl drains is MP, potions, scrolls, and item charges — not HP directly.** HP is the top-level gauge that forces a retreat decision; those consumables are what let a party manage it without spending the one thing that actually restores it: real rest.

**Recovery:** 1 HP per full day of rest, same rate and same restriction as MP recovery (Section 7) — no strenuous activity during that day, and this ties directly into the Rest & Recover downtime activity (Section 13).

**Death is expensive, not permanent** — reversing it costs something real, rather than an injury table softening what death means in the first place.

- **Undo** (Section 8's spell list) reverses a party-wide death — an arch-mage-tier Caster spell, not available at ordinary play.
- **Resurrection** happens through the Clone Vats — a Tier IV service, not a PC ability (Section 5's "commission major magic from an NPC, if available at all"). Whatever survives of the fallen character (remains, or a stored sample from some earlier arrangement) is regrown into a living body. Roll 2d6 when a character returns through the vats:

| 2d6 | Result |
|---|---|
| 2 | Amnesia — the character returns physically whole, but remembers nothing of their previous life |
| 3 | Roll one drawback (referee's choice, or borrow an unused Chimera drawback that fits the character) |
| 4-5 | Diminished — their ability (Section 2) functions at -1 until treated by a Cure or Heal effect; a visible scar or deformity, permanent |
| 6-8 | No problems |
| 9-10 | Gain +1 to a check outside their own defining ability, but skin, hair, or eyes take on an unnatural color or texture from now on |
| 11 | Roll one minor mutation (referee's call) |
| 12 | Roll one major mutation (referee's call) |

- **Reincarnation** has no separate mechanism — it's not part of the Clone Vats process and isn't otherwise addressed by these rules. Treat it as a rare, dramatic story event if it comes up, adjudicated case by case.

Healing potions, staves, and scrolls (Section 9) remain the everyday, non-emergency way to recover HP mid-dungeon.

### Saving Dice

The same roll used for non-combat actions (Section 11) also resolves resisting poison, disease, traps, and similar effects: 8+ on 2d6, same modifier table.

### Poison

Deals 1-3d6 damage, usually immediate. Slow-acting poison instead deals 1 hit per round until the full amount lands — a Cure spell stops further damage but doesn't undo what's already been dealt.

### Disease

Takes 1-3 days to manifest. An afflicted character loses all ability-based benefits (except ones granted by magic) and can't heal naturally until cured; magic and miracles still work normally. About 1-in-6 diseases are virulent — deadly within 1d6+1 days unless cured quickly.

# IV. Campaign Rules

## 13. Downtime

Time spent in town runs in weekly blocks. Each week, a character can pursue one activity:

| Activity | Time | Tier cost |
|---|---|---|
| Rest & Recover | 1 week | I (lodging only — doesn't change the 1 HP/day or 1 MP/day recovery rate) |
| Upkeep (just being in town) | 1 week | I |
| Carousing, rumors, research (non-spell) | 1 week | I |
| Hireling recruitment | 1 week | I |
| Training (Hunter, Ranger, Monk; Priest, for its combat-move milestones) | 1 week | II |
| Spell Research (Priest, Monk; occasionally Caster/Shaman for something unusual) | 1 week | II |
| Crafting | 1+ weeks | Varies — see the Tier Ladder below and Section 5 |

### The Tier Ladder

One rule generates every downtime cost rather than a table of special cases:
- **Buy or commission** a durable item from an NPC — the asset's own Tier (Section 5).
- **Self-craft it, or hire a one-time, non-storable effect** — the asset's Tier minus one.

### Training and Spell Research Never Gate the Guaranteed Floor

Every class keeps its own built-in rate of gaining new spells or maneuvers, whether or not a player ever spends downtime on either:

| Class type | Guaranteed floor |
|---|---|
| Full caster (Caster, Shaman) | New spell every level |
| Restricted caster (Monk) | New spell(s) at 3rd, 6th, and 9th |
| Combatant with maneuvers (Hunter, Ranger, Monk) | New maneuver at 3rd, 6th (and 1st for Hunter); Hunter's 9th is its own feature |
| Hybrid (Priest) | A move or a spell, player's choice, at 3rd, 6th, and 9th |

Training and Spell Research only add something *beyond* that floor — a bespoke technique outside the maneuver list, or a spell outside the normal progression. A character who never touches either still gets everything the floor promises, right on schedule. A level's milestone (Section 6) is often the natural moment to frame one of these activities in the fiction — the lesson happens at the same time the guild rank is earned.

### Multi-Week Crafting

Paying for a staff, wand, or ring doesn't finish the job on the spot. Pay the cost once, then roll weekly for completion — the referee can reuse the same weekly pass/fail roll that governs potion-brewing, adjusted up or down for how well-equipped the effort is (a caster with a tower and apprentices finishes faster than a lone hermit). Exact odds are left to the table.

## 14. Strongholds

At 9th level, a character can build a stronghold — a stable base, followers, and (usually) a trickle of income. Every stronghold, regardless of class, costs a **Tier IV asset** and takes about a month to build; this matches how Section 4.1 already categorizes "a small building" and "a castle" as Tier IV items, so there's no separate cost table needed here.

| Class | Stronghold | Followers |
|---|---|---|
| Hunter | Castle | 10 archers, 15 footmen |
| Delver | Thieves' den | 20 burglars, 2d6 informants |
| Caster | Tower | 1 alchemist, 2 apprentices, 15 footmen |
| Priest | Archive | Informants and a handful of loyal contacts, scaled down from Delver's |
| Shaman | Grove | 5 attendants, 15 archers |
| Ranger | Lodge | A small band of scouts and trackers |
| Monk | Temple | 15 acolytes |

Followers arrive within the first month and are fanatically loyal barring real abuse. ### Regional Stability

Once a stronghold exists, roll monthly:

| 2d6 | Result |
|---|---|
| 2-7 | Stable — revenue as normal |
| 8-9 | Destabilized — no revenue this month |
| 10-12 | Unstable — revenue lost, roll for an inciting incident below |

|---|---|
| 2-6 | Poor harvest or resource shortage |
| 7 | Monster or raider incursion |
| 8-12 | Neighboring war or uprising |

This is meant to write a campaign's background events for you — a bad month doesn't need to derail a session, but three bad months in a row is a plot hook the referee shouldn't ignore.

# V. Enemy Rules

## 17. Boss & Encounter Design

Guidance for the referee on building an encounter that delivers a real boss-fight or horde feeling, using the monster stats in Section 18.

### Level Gap

A monster's level and a party's level aren't meant to match. A solo monster at the party's own level is trivial — it dies in a few rounds and barely threatens anyone. A genuine solo boss should sit roughly 3-4 levels above the party, and should have either multiple attacks or a signature ability that functions like one — a single-attack monster stays trivial at almost any level gap.

### Telegraphed Signature Abilities

A boss with a big area attack (a dragon's breath, a manticore's spike volley) works like this:

- **Declared at the start of the round, before initiative** — the same telegraph convention every caster in the game already follows (Section 7).
- **Resolved exactly as each creature's own entry describes it.** A Manticore's spikes are an automatic hit with no save, dividing damage among everyone within range. A breathing creature follows the dragon-breath damage scale (Section 18), save for half where noted.
- **A natural 12 on Smash interrupts** the ability that round.
- **Frequency is capped by tier** — 1 use for a young-tier threat, 2 for mature, 3 for ancient, never more than once every other round.

### Magic Resistance

Sustained spellcasting is the single biggest threat to a boss encounter's pacing. Any monster meant to function as a real boss should carry some form of resistance or counter to weaker magic, the same way the setting's most dangerous named threats already do.

### Testing Encounters

A party reaching a boss has usually been worn down by everything before it — HP recovers slowly (Section 12), so it's a whole-dungeon resource, not a per-fight one. Test boss math at roughly **half HP and half MP**, not full — a telegraphed area attack that's manageable at full health can be a near-certain kill after a realistic run of prior encounters.

### Hordes

A group of identical creatures gets its difficulty spread from three existing levers: HP per creature, armor, and weapon choice. A chainmail-wearing version of an ordinary monster is a meaningfully tougher fight than the base version.

### Level 4-6 Descriptions

**GRASS ASSASSIN** ambush from concealment, striking once with automatic surprise before retreating to re-hide.

**WOOLY GATOR** are identical to an ordinary crocodile in every mechanical respect, just furred and amphibious.

**ODOGARON** hunt in fast pairs; two attacking the same target together get +1 to hit.

**CORPRUS-KIN** are living things warped by long exposure to a dead magic-engine's leaking power. A touch forces a save (as Disease, Section 12) or the corruption spreads.

**DODONGO** are large armored lizards — tameable as beasts of burden when well-fed, dangerous and territorial when hungry or cornered.

**GIGANTOAD** attack with a heavy tongue-lash or body-slam — raw physical force, nothing more.

**ENFORCER** are the tougher fighters within a hostile clan or bandit group, encountered alongside ordinary Bandits, never alone — roughly one per ten rank-and-file.

**HOOK HORROR** are large bipedal creatures with a chitinous exoskeleton and hooked, blade-like talons in place of hands. Blind, but detect surroundings by sound (as the Goblin clan trait, Section 3) — loud noise disrupts this the same way.

**UMBER HULK** has a confusing gaze: anyone meeting its eyes within 20' must save or suffer the Jinx spell's effect for 1d6 rounds.

**INTELLECT DEVOURER** uses the Edimmu's possession mechanic directly: a failed save means no healing and no benefit from armor, losing 1 hit per day until death, at which point the creature takes the body.

**GIBBERING MOUTHER** babbles constantly; anyone within 20' saves each round or suffers Jinx's effect, cumulative with repeated exposure. Attacks with a simple bite between babbling rounds.

**YANMA** are large, fast dragonflies with a single stinging bite — a wilderness hazard, not a serious threat alone.

**SHALK** spit acid or fire at range (20', same damage as their sting) instead of attacking in melee, otherwise a stationary ambush predator.

**DURZOG** hunt in packs like wolves, otherwise mechanically identical.

**SAHAGIN** are tribal amphibious raiders fighting in organized bands — treat as a Merman with a land-capable move rate.

**COEURL** are fast, evasive predators: attackers need a 10 or better to hit them (as the Copy spell's effect), excluding automatic-hit magic.

**ZU** are massive aggressive birds, sometimes hunted as trophies — a claw-and-peck attack, nothing more.

**MALBORO** has a signature "Bad Breath": once per encounter, declared before initiative, a cone that forces a save against Slow, Stun, and Jinx simultaneously rather than dealing direct damage — contested the same way as any telegraphed boss ability (Section 17).

**OCHU/OTYUGH** has a signature grab-and-crush attack dealing a flat 3 hits, ignoring armor entirely.

**GRELL** are eyeless floating aberrations that sense prey by vibration. They attack with barbed tentacles (paralytic venom, as the Stun spell on a hit) and a separate beak bite once a target is grappled.
