# Casting Spells

The actual mechanics of casting are unchanged from the base game. The one real departure is when a spell has to be committed to — everything else below is the original system running as-is.

**Cost.** Every spell costs a flat 1 MP to cast, regardless of how powerful the spell is. There's no scaling cost for bigger effects — power comes from which spells a class has access to and at what level, not from paying more per cast.

**Running dry.** Once a caster's MP pool is fully spent, they can't cast again until they recover some back. Magic items — scrolls, wands, staves, rings — still work perfectly normally even when their user is completely out of personal MP; item charges are a separate resource from a caster's own pool.

**Recovery.** MP comes back at a rate of 1 per full day of rest, and that day has to be spent on nothing more strenuous than rest and quiet study — no adventuring, no travel, no fighting. This is the same rest that recovers HP (Section 11) and ties directly into the Rest & Recover downtime activity (Section 12).

**Declaring a spell.** Every spell is declared **before initiative is rolled**, not cast as a normal action on your turn. This applies to every caster in the game, at every level, with no exceptions — a visible telegraph rather than a surprise. It's what makes a boss's signature spell (Section 14) something a party can actually react to, and it applies just as much to a 1st-level Caster's first-ever spell as it does to an archdemon's.

**Using magic items.** Reading a scroll, activating a wand, or drawing on a staff never costs MP — the item supplies its own power. Whether a class can use a given kind of magic item at all is set by that class's entry in Section 4.
